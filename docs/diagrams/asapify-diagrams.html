From a satellite pixel to a Telegram alert

How ASAPify watches the peat of Sumatra and Kalimantan for fire, and where each part runs on Google Cloud.

Google Cloud AI Builder Cup 2026. Development frozen on 18 October 2026; these diagrams describe that state.

What ASAPify watches

Peat burns slowly and smokes for weeks. ASAPify looks for unusual heat over mapped peat and tells people in a Telegram group when it finds some.

65
peat regions in 11 provinces of Sumatra and Kalimantan
2

15 min
between two ratings of every region
20–30
minutes from a satellite scan to a status in the app
2 km
satellite pixels, each one scored and marked on or off mapped peat
SafeNo fire-like heat in the satellites that see the region.
WarningMedium anomaly: closer watching, no AI check.
AlertStrong anomaly: Gemini checks the evidence, an operator decides.
Not observableCloud over 60 % in both views, or no data. Never counted as safe.

Three paths through the system

Blue squares are Google Cloud services; grey ones are outside it.

Every 15 minutes

The engine rates all 65 regions.

  1. Cloud Scheduler wakes the workerA private call with OIDC, at minutes 5, 20, 35 and 50.
  2. It reads the newest scanHimawari-9 and GK2A from NOAA Open Data on AWS, the one both have published.
  3. asapify-notify rates every regionAnomaly, fusion, dryness and sun shifts, Safe to Alert.
  4. Firestore keeps the resultStatus, slot record, status changes and the day archive in one batch.
  5. New Alerts get the agentGemini runs while time is left in the tick; the rest wait for the next one.

Someone opens the map

No sign-in; the CDN answers most requests.

  1. The browser asks Firebase HostingThe app's files, then /api/v1/public/regions.
  2. The Hosting CDN answersFrom its cache for up to 120 s; a past day for 1 day.
  3. Otherwise asapify-api reads FirestoreWithout operator emails, notes or actions.
  4. MapLibre draws the mapRegions, hot pixels and VIIRS on the OpenFreeMap basemap.

An alert is published

An operator, or the 2-hour rule.

  1. The operator signs in with GoogleFirebase Auth; the API checks role=operator.
  2. One Firestore transactionDecision, audit entry and alert together; a second decision is refused.
  3. Cloud Tasks carries one job per groupTo the private worker, with OIDC; chat ids never enter a task.
  4. Telegram receives the postsendMessage in the group. Every minute the sweep queues what is still pending; it never resends blindly.
03

Backend

ktp-asapify-be: FastAPI on Cloud Run, the risk engine and the Gemini agent.

Rating one slot, every 15 minutes

POST /_internal/engine/geo-tick on the worker, in asapify_engine/geo.py. Starting values, not yet calibrated.

1Pick the slot

The newest 10-minute scan both satellites have published. Agent runs left over from the last tick go first.

2Read both satellites

Himawari-9 B07 and B13, GK2A SW038 and IR105, from NOAA Open Data on AWS (satellites.py).

3Contextual anomaly

ΔBT = 3.9 µm − 10.4 µm against the clear pixels of the same cell: ≥ 3 K and ≥ 3σ, and 3.9 µm up ≥ 2 K and ≥ 3σ.

4Cloud check

Cloud read from the 10.4 µm channel.

Over 60 % in both views: Not observable.
5Fuse the views

ΔT is the mean of the views that see the region, weighted by clear share; the stronger view joins as a second attribute.

6Shift for air and sun

ΔT counted = ΔT + 1 K·tanh(dry z / 2) − 1 K·sin(sun elevation); humidity against its NASA POWER normal.

7Fuzzy rules

Trapezoids give Safe, Warning, Alert; Fuzzy MAUT weighs the mean 0.6 and the strongest view up to 0.4. Safe < 1.95 K ≤ Warning < 6.95 K ≤ Alert.

8Support

VIIRS hotspots near a hot pixel and NASA POWER skin temperature can only raise Alert, never lower it.

9Save it

State machine, slot record, status change and the day archive, in one Firestore batch (fsm.py, repo.py).

10Ask the agent

A region that entered Alert queues a Gemini run.

Once per region in 24 h, 20 runs a day, 60 s of the tick left.
NASA POWER tick, every 15 min: new hours per region, dryness z and the humidity normal (ingest.py). Open-Meteo stands in for humidity while POWER is more than 4 days late.

From Alert to the Telegram group

verifier.py, publication.py, auto_publish.py and the notification modules.

The agentgeospatial_analyst calls fetch_firms_data, looks at a true- and a false-colour GIBS image, and answers in YAML. The backend checks its numbers against what the tool returned; a Confirmed without smoke or a burn scar in clear images from the last 24 h becomes Needs review.
The decisionPublish or reject, in one transaction with the audit entry. A second decision is refused; reject still works after 12 h.
The safety netEvery minute the sweep reads only the Alert regions and publishes a Confirmed nobody decided on in 2 hours, as automatic.

Limits at the freeze

  • Not calibrated. Every threshold is a starting value, checked against VIIRS on one night slot only.
  • A 2 km pixel sees large fires. Small or smouldering ones often stay below it, where VIIRS at 375 m still sees them.
  • Cloud hides the ground from both satellites; such regions are Not observable, never Safe.
  • Not an operational warning system. Latency and reach in the field were never measured; Telegram's "accepted" is not "read".
  • Gaps in the archive. A tick rates only the newest slot and never backfills: at most 96 of 144 scans a day.

What a utility is not

A Fuzzy MAUT utility is a match score, and the agent's confidence is its own score. Neither is a probability of fire.

The peat map in use is incomplete, so a fire anywhere in a cell counts, marked on or off mapped peat.
